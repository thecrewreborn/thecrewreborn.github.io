The Crew Reborn

Servers offline since 31 March 2024

Bought.Switched off.

On 31 March 2024 Ubisoft shut down the servers of The Crew. The game cannot start without them, so since that day nobody gets past the start screen, on disc or download, on console or PC. This page tells what happened next and why The Crew Reborn exists.

Symbolic image: server racks. Photo: Victor Grigas, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

What happened

The Crew came out in December 2014 for PC and consoles: an open world across the whole United States, made for driving with others. The game asks the publisher's servers for everything, even for the story you play alone. That design decided its fate.

  1. 14 Dec 2023

    Taken off sale

    Ubisoft removes The Crew from every store and announces that the servers will shut down on 31 March 2024.

  2. 31 Mar 2024

    The servers go dark

    Without its servers the game stops at the start screen on every platform. Ubisoft does not release an offline mode or the server software.

  3. April 2024

    The outcry

    YouTuber Ross Scott launches the campaign Stop Killing Games, with The Crew as its main example. Weeks later, players report that Ubisoft has removed the game from their Ubisoft Connect libraries.

  4. Sept 2025

    Fans step in

    A fan team releases a server emulator for the PC version. More on it below.

  5. Oct 2025

    An offline mode, for the sequel

    Ubisoft gives The Crew 2 an offline mode. The first The Crew gets none.

  6. 26 Jan 2026

    1.29 million signatures

    The European citizens' initiative “Stop Destroying Videogames” reaches the European Commission with 1,294,188 verified signatures.

  7. Apr – May 2026

    Brussels and Strasbourg

    A public hearing in the European Parliament on 16 April, a debate in the plenary on 21 May.

  8. 16 Jun 2026

    The Commission answers

    At this stage the Commission will not propose a legal duty to keep games playable after sales end. By the end of 2026 it wants to start talks with the industry about a voluntary code of conduct.

  9. Today

    Still no way back

    Ubisoft offers no way to play the first The Crew, and no law requires one.

A loud outcry

1,294,188verified signatures from EU citizens for “Stop Destroying Videogames”

The shutdown struck a nerve far beyond racing fans. Stop Killing Games made The Crew the case everyone points to: a game people paid for and can no longer play. In the EU, more than a million people signed the citizens' initiative. Its organisers met Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen and Commissioner Michael McGrath in Brussels and argued their case in the European Parliament.

For the first The Crew, nothing changed. The Commission relies on voluntary rules from the industry, and Ubisoft has announced no way back for this game.

A million signatures later, the game still stops at the start screen.

Another fan project

Fans moved faster than the publisher. In September 2025 another fan team released a server emulator that runs the PC version again, with a local server on your own computer. That took years of work and gave PC players their game back.

For many other players the door stays shut:

  • PC onlyThere is no version for PlayStation or Xbox. If you own The Crew on a console, you still cannot play it.
  • No multiplayer yetThe game runs as an offline mode. An online mode is announced but not released, so you cannot drive with friends.
  • Closed sourceThe source code is not public. Nobody outside the team can check it, build on it or carry it on if the team stops.

As of November 2026, according to the project's own website. The Crew Reborn is not affiliated with that project.

So: a project of its own

Console players had no way back. Nobody could drive together. And because the code is closed, nobody else could add either. That left one option: build a separate server, from the ground up, in the open.

The Crew Reborn runs on the PS4, the PS5 and the PC. Players on both platforms meet in one world and drive in one crew. The server runs on any computer in your home network, Windows, macOS or Linux, and your save lives on your own disk.

The code is open source under the GPL 3. If I stop working on it one day, the code stays public and anyone can carry it on. Nobody can switch this server off for you, me included.
